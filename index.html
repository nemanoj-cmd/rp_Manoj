Effect of UV Light Radiation on Planarian Regeneration

Research Project

Background

Planarians are well known for their regenerative properties, allowing them to regrow parts of their body when harmed. This is due to the presence of collagen and stem cells, which allow for rapid regeneration rates. This study looks at how long-term exposure to ultraviolet (UV) light can affect regenerative processes, regarding survival rates and movement. Over a three-week period, brown planarians were exposed to Ultraviolet A and Ultraviolet B light, serving as independent variables, over varying durations of time.

Rationale

Similarly to humans, Planarians utilize collagen to regulate the creation of these cells and play a large part in the regeneration process. Because of these features, planarians are often used to model cell growth in other organisms, including humans.

Procedure

To analyze the planarian’s regenerative ability after exposure, the time required for a planarian to reach the shaded side of a dish was recorded as the dependent variable. Certain treatment groups had a high mortality rate, supporting the hypothesis that UV light exposure is detrimental to regenerative abilities, while others had a minimal effect on surviving planarians. After experimentation, statistical analysis revealed a significant difference between various surviving groups. Those that underwent UVA exposure had slower times than those that underwent UVB lighting. The experimentation revealed lasting damage, and can be used to model UV exposure in humans.
